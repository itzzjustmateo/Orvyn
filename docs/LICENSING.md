# Licensing

Current Orvyn application code and documentation are offered under the custom [Orvyn Free Redistribution License 1.0](../LICENSE), where the copyright holders have the necessary rights to do so.

## What the current license permits

- Use Orvyn personally or within a business.
- Read and modify the covered source.
- Share original or modified copies free of charge, keeping the license and notices.

## What requires separate written permission

- Selling or renting covered Orvyn copies.
- Charging for downloads or subscription delivery of copies.
- Making copies available only after a payment, purchase, or required donation.
- Bundling covered Orvyn material into a paid product or distribution.

Independent support and consulting may be charged for when obtaining the software does not require buying those services. The full license controls; this summary does not replace it.

## Earlier MIT releases

Earlier Orvyn copies were offered under MIT. This change does not take away those existing permissions, including the permission to sell MIT-covered copies. A person may continue to use the earlier MIT material under its original terms. The new restriction applies to material validly released under the new license; changing a license file cannot retroactively restrict an earlier grant.

The [historical MIT notice](../apps/manager/packaging/licenses/ORVYN-LEGACY-MIT-LICENSE.txt) is retained for previously MIT-covered material. Do not remove required notices from existing code or dependencies.

## Third-party components and packages

Model weights, llamafile, and dependency code retain their own licenses. The Orvyn restriction does not replace or reduce independently granted third-party rights. Keep the relevant [third-party notices](../apps/manager/packaging/licenses) when distributing packages.

Existing installer EXEs have not been rebuilt by this documentation change. Rebuild packages to include the new application license and installer license screen. Previously distributed copies keep the terms under which they were supplied.

This license is source-available rather than OSI-approved open source: the [Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd) requires freedom to sell and redistribute software. The [MIT text](https://opensource.org/license/mit) expressly permits selling copies.
